## 🚫 What NOT to Do During Recording
To maintain a high-quality presentation standard, avoid these common recording mistakes:
1. **Never use a high-DPI mouse pointer path:** Do not make rapid, erratic mouse movements or wiggle the cursor to show where you are looking. Let your hover effects do the pointing.
2. **Do not speak over page transitions:** Wait for a page load or scroll animation to complete *before* explaining the next point, otherwise the visual and audio tracks will conflict.
3. **Avoid system notifications:** Disable Windows notifications (turn on "Focus Assist") and close unnecessary tabs. A sudden Slack ping or personal email popup instantly ruins a demo.
4. **Don't use low-quality microphone audio:** Use a headset or dedicated mic rather than your laptop's built-in microphone to prevent background fan noise or echo.
5. **Never skip mobile view:** Hackathon judges test responsiveness first. Failing to show mobile layout signals an incomplete stylesheet.
6. **Avoid long silences or filler words:** Write down a script (like the ones above) to avoid saying "uhm", "like", or "basically".

## 🎥 Camera & Cursor Techniques
Make a standard screencast look like a high-budget commercial with these techniques:
* **The "Slow-In, Slow-Out" Cursor:** Move the cursor slowly toward interactive elements, decelerating right before hovering. This allows the hover effect to trigger gracefully without looking rushed.
* **The Infinity Loop Sweeping:** While explaining the hero section, move the cursor in a large, gentle horizontal figure-eight ($\infty$) to show the radial mouse glow follower without obstructing the main text.
* **Scroll Speed Control:** Use a mouse wheel with high scroll-smoothing or use your keyboard's arrow keys / spacebar to scroll down. Never drag the scrollbar on the right side of the screen.
* **The Click-and-Hold Ripple:** When clicking buttons (like the CTA or Join Future button), click and hold the left mouse button for half a second before releasing. This highlights the ripple animation expanding outward.
* **High Frame-Rate Capturing:** Record at `1080p` at `60 FPS` (using OBS Studio or similar). Cyberspace animations and CSS transitions look choppy at 30 FPS.

## 🏆 Judge's Grading Tips
To score maximum marks from engineering and design judges:
* **Highlight "Vanilla" Tech Stack:** Judges love clean code without bloated frameworks. Explicitly state: *"Built with semantic HTML5, vanilla CSS3, and standard ES6 JavaScript."*
* **Explain the Scroll Observer:** Mention that animations are *performance-optimized* using the browser's `IntersectionObserver` API rather than scroll event listeners, preventing layout thrashing.
* **Callout UX Design Details:** Point out the *glassmorphism* filters (`backdrop-filter: blur()`), cohesive color system (neon cyan, deep violet, carbon background), and accessibility features (`aria-labels` on buttons/hamburger).
* **Prove Responsive Optimization:** By opening Chrome DevTools and resizing the viewport on screen, you prove that media queries are fully responsive and not just static layouts.
